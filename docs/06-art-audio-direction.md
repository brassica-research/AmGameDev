# 06 — Art & Audio Direction

## Look: "Painted from life"

The opposite pole from *Tears of Metal*'s hand-drawn cartoon: we target
**grounded realism with a painterly finish** — as if Don Troiani's
battle paintings and the Peale/Trumbull portrait tradition were rendered
in engine. Realistic proportions, materials, and lighting; restrained
post-processing that leans toward oil-on-canvas value structure rather
than photorealistic sterility.

### Visual pillars

1. **Wool, linen, leather, mud.** Uniforms are *worn*: faded madder red,
   unmatched Continental browns and blues (most of the army never had the
   regulation uniform — hunting shirts everywhere), patched elbows, bare
   feet where the record says bare feet. Cleanliness signals: French
   troops at Yorktown gleam; the Continentals do not. That contrast is a
   story told without a word.
2. **Powder smoke owns the battlefield.** Volumetric smoke is the
   signature VFX investment. After the third volley the field is a gray
   sea with bayonets in it — beautiful, terrifying, and tactically real.
3. **Natural light discipline.** Time-of-day and weather from the
   historical record (fog on the East River Aug 30, 1776; the Trenton
   nor'easter; Monmouth's white heat; Germantown fog). No orange-teal
   grading; palettes come from the paintings.
4. **Period-correct detail budget.** Brown Bess & Charleville muskets,
   correct drill (the 1764 Manual Exercise → von Steuben's simplified
   drill after Valley Forge — the *animation set literally changes*
   after the drill questline), correct flags including the many
   regimental colors that weren't the stars and stripes.
5. **Readability without HUD-noise.** Formations read by silhouette,
   colors, and drum/fife audio cues; minimal diegetic-styled UI (orders
   as officer voice lines, unit state readable from posture and dressing
   of the line).

### Reference set

- **Paintings:** Don Troiani (uniforms & battle staging), John Trumbull
  (composition, faces), Charles Willson Peale (portraits from life),
  Xavier della Gatta's 1782 Paoli/Germantown paintings (eyewitness-derived).
- **Film:** *Barry Lyndon* (light & line battle), *The Crossing* (2000),
  *Turn: Washington's Spies* (texture), *Master and Commander* (period
  materiality & crew-as-family tone).
- **Places:** Minute Man NHP, Valley Forge, Yorktown battlefield —
  terrain from survey data where obtainable (see 08).

## Audio

- **The drum is the UI.** Real field music conveyed orders; ours does
  too — each command has its authentic drum signal, and enemy drums are
  intelligence you learn to read.
- **Period music, sourced.** Fife & drum repertoire ("The White Cockade,"
  "Road to Boston," "Yankee Doodle" — which the British played in mockery
  and the Americans reclaimed at Yorktown: the game scores that reversal),
  William Billings' *Chester* as the Patriot hymn-motif, "The World
  Turned Upside Down" for the surrender. Score grows from these seeds —
  solo fiddle/fife in 1775 to full ensemble by 1781.
- **Musketry with weight.** A volley is a single terrifying *crack-roll*,
  not a movie popcorn rattle; artillery arrives as pressure you feel in
  the mix. Silence is used ruthlessly (the night digging at Dorchester,
  the unloaded-musket assaults).
- **Voice:** period-regional accents (see 04); commands shouted by your
  actual officer characters, positional in the mix — losing an officer
  literally makes your brigade harder to *hear*.

## UI/UX tone

Parchment, iron gall ink, letterpress type (Caslon — the Declaration's
face), engraved-map campaign screens in the style of period cartography
(Faden's 1777 battle maps). The muster roll looks like a muster roll.
No sci-fi glow anywhere in the game.
