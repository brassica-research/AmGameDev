# 01 — Vision

## One-sentence pitch

*Tears of Metal*'s "fight as the hero, command the battalion, mourn every
lost soldier" loop, transplanted from cartoon-medieval Scotland to a
grounded, realistic American Revolution — where the campaign map is the
actual historical record from the Boston Massacre to Washington's
inauguration.

## What we take from Tears of Metal

*Tears of Metal* (Paper Cult, 2026) is a hack-and-slash roguelite in which
the player is simultaneously a frontline fighter and the commander of a
battalion that fights alongside them. The elements worth carrying over:

1. **The dual role.** You swing a weapon yourself, but your real power is
   the formation moving with you. Individual skill and command decisions
   both matter, moment to moment.
2. **Persistent soldiers.** Soldiers are recruited, named, gain skills
   across campaigns, and die permanently. The emotional core of the game is
   the muster roll.
3. **The hub between battles.** A place where you spend hard-won resources
   on recruitment, training, equipment, and morale before marching out again.
4. **Run structure with objectives.** Battles are bounded sorties with a
   primary objective and opportunistic sub-objectives that feed the economy.
5. **Co-op shaped holes.** Up to four commanders on one field, each leading
   their own wing — designed in from the start, shipped later.

## What we deliberately change

| Tears of Metal | Twilight's Gleaming |
|---|---|
| Hand-drawn, cartoony, exaggerated | Realistic, painterly-grounded; mud, wool, powder smoke |
| Fictional Scotland, fantasy metal | Documented events, real people, real places, 1770–1789 |
| Randomized sandbox battlefields | Authored historical battlefields (randomization lives in a separate Skirmish mode) |
| Roguelite run-reset as the core | An authored campaign as the core; roguelite texture *inside* missions (dynamic weather, shifting objectives, permadeath in your ranks) |
| Melee-dominant medieval combat | Black-powder combat: volleys, reloads, bayonets, artillery — tension comes from the 15–20 seconds a reload takes |
| Victory is always the goal | History is the spine: some missions are about *surviving* a defeat with your regiment intact |

## Design pillars

1. **History is the spine, your army is the story.** We do not rewrite
   outcomes of the war. The player's agency is the fate of the men and
   women in their brigade — who lives, who deserts, who freezes at Valley
   Forge, who is standing at Yorktown. Losing Long Island is history;
   losing *your entire regiment* on Long Island is on you.
2. **The volley is sacred.** Black-powder combat is slow, loud, and
   terrifying, and the game must feel that way. One synchronized volley
   should feel more powerful than any button-mash. Reload time is the
   fundamental rhythm of combat.
3. **Weight over spectacle.** No glowing swords, no health bars floating
   over heads by default, no arcade score popups. Smoke, drums, officers'
   shouted commands, and the line holding (or breaking) are the spectacle.
4. **Primary sources on screen.** Cutscenes and codex entries quote the
   actual letters, diaries, and speeches (see `08-historical-sources.md`).
   When Washington speaks before Trenton, the words are Thomas Paine's,
   because that is what was actually read to the troops.
5. **The cost is the content.** Smallpox, expiring enlistments, worthless
   Continental currency, mutiny, desertion — the administrative agony of
   keeping an army alive *is* the meta-game, not a menu abstraction.

## The player fantasy

Not "be George Washington." You are a fictional-but-plausible line officer —
a composite in the tradition of Joseph Plumb Martin's memoir — who rises
from a Massachusetts militia company in 1775 to command of a Continental
brigade by 1781. Washington, Greene, Knox, and Lafayette are characters you
serve under and alongside; historical figures do the things they actually
did, on the days they actually did them. This keeps the historical record
intact while giving the player a life inside it.

(A named-commander mode — playing key historical engagements *as* Morgan at
Cowpens, *as* Washington at Monmouth — is planned as a separate "Command
Moments" mode; see `03-campaign.md`.)

## Tone references

- *Turn: Washington's Spies* (AMC) — grounded texture of the period
- *Barry Lyndon* (Kubrick) — natural light, the terrifying formality of line battle
- *The Crossing* (2000) — Trenton done with gravity
- *1776* by David McCullough — narrative tension from real correspondence
- John Trumbull / Charles Willson Peale / Don Troiani paintings — visual anchor
