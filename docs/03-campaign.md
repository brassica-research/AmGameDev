# 03 — Campaign: *From the Green to the Republic*

Three acts, 1770–1789. Every mission is anchored to a documented event on
its real date and ground. Mission types:

- **BATTLE** — brigade combat on an authored historical field
- **EXTRACTION** — survive/withdraw with as much of the army as possible
- **SIEGE** — multi-phase works, artillery, assault
- **VIGNETTE** — playable non-battle scene (stealth, standoff, crowd, march)
- **HUB** — winter-quarters chapter with its own systems
- **CINEMATIC** — cutscene chapter (see `05-cutscenes.md`)
- **CHOICE** — the campaign branches by *detachment orders*: which real
  wing of the army your fictional brigade serves in. Both paths are
  historical; they reconverge. History never changes — your vantage does.

## ACT I — THE GATHERING STORM (1770–1775)

*You are a civilian, then a militiaman. The act teaches crowd, stealth,
and skirmish play before the first true battle, and makes the war feel
like the last resort it was.*

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | The player's part |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------------------|
| 1.0 | **Blood on King Street** | Mar 5, 1770 | Boston | CINEMATIC → VIGNETTE | Opening cutscene (the Massacre), then playable aftermath: carry a wounded neighbor home through a city on the edge. Tutorial: movement, crowd pressure, no weapon. |
| 1.1 | **The Gaspee** | Jun 9–10, 1772 | Narragansett Bay, RI | VIGNETTE | Night longboat raid on the grounded revenue schooner *Gaspee*. Stealth/rowing/boarding tutorial. Historical constraint honored: Lt. Dudingston is wounded, no one is killed, the ship burns. |
| 1.2 | **Saltwater Tea** | Dec 16, 1773 | Griffin's Wharf, Boston | VIGNETTE | The Tea Party as a *discipline* mission: destroy the tea, harm no one, steal nothing (a participant who pocketed tea was historically stripped and shamed — that's a fail state). Teaches the restraint the Sons demanded. |
| 1.3 | **The Powder Alarm** | Sep 1, 1774 | Middlesex County, MA | VIGNETTE | Ride and muster: rumor says the Regulars are out. Race to raise your town's company — meet the men whose names will fill your muster roll. Tutorial: militia roster introduced. |
| 1.4 | **Leslie's Standoff** | Feb 26, 1775 | Salem, MA | VIGNETTE | Col. Leslie's Regulars come for cannon; the drawbridge is up. A standoff won with nerve, not fire — hold the militia's line *without* a shot (morale/discipline mechanics under maximum tension). War almost starts seven weeks early; it doesn't. |
| 1.5 | **Nineteenth of April** | Apr 19, 1775 | Lexington → Concord → Battle Road | BATTLE | Triptych: Lexington Green (scripted tragedy — you cannot win, only witness and survive), North Bridge (first ordered volley — "the shot heard round the world" is the game's first Present–Fire), Battle Road (running wall-to-wall skirmish back to Boston). Full combat tutorial arc. |
| 1.6 | **The Redoubt** (Act finale) | Jun 17, 1775 | Breed's Hill, Charlestown | BATTLE → EXTRACTION | Hold the redoubt through two British assaults with brutal volley work; when ammunition runs out on the third, conduct a fighting withdrawal. Featuring Prescott ("whites of their eyes"), Peter Salem and Salem Poor in the ranks, the death of Dr. Joseph Warren (cutscene). A "defeat" that proves the army can stand. |
| 1.H | **Cambridge Camp** | Summer 1775 | Cambridge, MA | HUB | First hub. Washington arrives July 3 to take command — your first meeting. Convert your militia company into something like an army. |

## ACT II — THE WAR (1775–1781)

### Chapter 1: Boston to the Brink

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | The player's part |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------------------|
| 2.1 | **The Noble Train** | Dec 1775–Jan 1776 | Ticonderoga → Cambridge | VIGNETTE | Escort Knox's 60 tons of cannon across frozen rivers and the Berkshires. Logistics-as-gameplay: ice, oxen, sledges, a sinking gun recovered from the Mohawk. |
| 2.2 | **One Night's Work** | Mar 4–5, 1776 | Dorchester Heights | VIGNETTE → CINEMATIC | Fortify the heights in silence in a single night (prefab chandeliers and fascines — real engineering trick). Dawn cutscene: Howe sees the works; March 17, the British sail away. A bloodless victory to teach that position > slaughter. |
| 2.3 | **The Old Line** | Aug 27, 1776 | Brooklyn — Gowanus Road | BATTLE (unwinnable front) | The flank is turned via the unguarded Jamaica Pass. Fight the delaying action alongside the Maryland 400's repeated charges at the Old Stone House. Objective: buy minutes, not victory. |
| 2.4 | **The Water Is Smooth** | Aug 29–30, 1776 | Brooklyn ferry line | EXTRACTION | The night evacuation. Man the rearguard trenches as Glover's Marblehead mariners row 9,000 men across the East River in silence and fog. Every soldier of yours who boards is saved; the fog (historically real) rolls in as a mechanic. The purest *Tears of Metal*-style extraction in the game. |
| 2.5 | **Rally at Harlem** | Sep 15–16, 1776 | Kip's Bay → Harlem Heights | BATTLE | Day 1: rout (scripted — survive it). Day 2: the counterpunch at Harlem Heights, Knowlton's Rangers flanking through the Hollow Way. Knowlton falls. First taste of *winning* in the open field. |
| 2.6 | **The Long Retreat** | Nov–Dec 1776 | The Jerseys | VIGNETTE (attrition march) | The army dissolves around you: enlistments expiring, shoes gone, Congress fled. March-management gameplay; keep your company's core together. Fort Washington's fall (Nov 16) arrives as devastating news. Ends with Paine's *The American Crisis* read aloud (cutscene — see 05). |
| 2.7 | **Victory or Death** | Dec 25–26, 1776 | McConkey's Ferry → Trenton | BATTLE | The crossing (ice, nor'easter, Glover's boats again), the nine-mile night march (keep the column closed up, muskets dry), the two-column dawn assault. Knox's guns rake King and Queen Streets; Rall falls; ~900 Hessians taken. The password is the mission title. |
| 2.8 | **The Bridge at Assunpink** | Jan 2, 1777 | Trenton | BATTLE | Hold the stone bridge at dusk against three assaults — then slip away by night, campfires burning, wheels muffled, to strike Princeton. |
| 2.9 | **Princeton** | Jan 3, 1777 | Princeton, NJ | BATTLE | Mercer's brigade breaks; Washington rides between the lines within thirty yards of British muskets to rally it (scripted setpiece you fight inside). Mercer dies of his wounds — the muster roll's first general. |
| 2.H1 | **Morristown** | Winter 1777 | Morristown, NJ | HUB | Smallpox inoculation decision (Washington's real order — a calculated gamble you make for your own brigade). Recruiting for the "new army" of three-year men. |

### Chapter 2: Two Roads to Valley Forge — **CHOICE**

*September 1777. Your brigade receives detachment orders. Both paths are
real; Glover's brigade genuinely marched north. The paths reconverge at
Valley Forge.*

**Path A — With Washington (Philadelphia campaign)**

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | Notes |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------|
| 2.A1 | **Brandywine** | Sep 11, 1777 | Chadds Ford / Birmingham Meeting House | BATTLE | Flanked again (Howe's long march); hold Birmingham Hill through successive positions. Lafayette wounded rallying troops — you're beside him. Defeat with honor; Philadelphia falls. |
| 2.A2 | **Fog at Germantown** | Oct 4, 1777 | Germantown, PA | BATTLE | The audacious four-column counterattack that nearly worked. Fog + friendly fire as core mechanics; the Chew House stone strongpoint decision (bypass or reduce?) mirrors the real council argument. |

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | Notes |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------|

### Chapter 3: The Crucible

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | The player's part |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------------------|

| 2.11 | **The Rhode Island Line** *(optional)* | Aug 29, 1778 | Aquidneck Island, RI | BATTLE | Fight beside the 1st Rhode Island Regiment — the largely Black and Indigenous regiment — repelling three Hessian assaults during the withdrawal. | 2.12 | **Bayonets Only** | Jul 16, 1779 | Stony Point, Hudson River | VIGNETTE → BATTLE | Wayne's midnight assault: muskets *unloaded* by order, axemen ahead, watchword "The fort's our own." ## ACT III — A MORE PERFECT UNION (1782–1789)

*The war is won; the Revolution isn't. Act III shifts genre weight from
battle to tension — the army unpaid, the union fragile. Shorter, heavier.*

| # | Mission | Date | Place | Type | The player's part |
|---|---------|------|-------|------|-------------------|
| 3.1 | **The Idle Camp** | 1782–83 | Newburgh, NY | HUB | Peace rumored, pay years in arrears, officers furious. Hub systems turn dark: keep discipline in an army with nothing to do and every reason to march on Congress. |
| 3.2 | **The Newburgh Address** | Mar 15, 1783 | The Temple, New Windsor | VIGNETTE (assembly) | The near-coup, playable as an influence scene among the officers you've served with for eight years. Washington's spectacles moment (see 05) breaks it. You choose where you stand *before* he enters — the game remembers your answer even though history supplies the outcome. |
| 3.3 | **Evacuation Day** | Nov 25, 1783 | New York City | VIGNETTE | March into liberated New York behind Knox; the greased flagpole at the Battery (a real, wry detail) as the act's one moment of pure joy. |
| 3.4 | **Farewell** | Dec 4 & Dec 23, 1783 | Fraunces Tavern / Annapolis | CINEMATIC | Washington's farewell to his officers, then the resignation of his commission at Annapolis — the scene that made the republic real. |
| 3.5 | **Home** | 1784–86 | Player's home colony | VIGNETTE | Demobilized. Your land certificates trade at pennies; old soldiers drift into debt. Quiet, personal, Joseph Plumb Martin-toned chapter — the seed of what comes next. |
| 3.6 | **Brothers' Blood** | Jan 25, 1787 | Springfield Armory, MA | VIGNETTE → STANDOFF | Shays' Rebellion. Men from your own muster roll may stand on *both* sides (the roster system makes this literal). The armory confrontation: grapeshot over open field, four dead — the game's hardest scene precisely because the enemy is your own veterans, and the fight barely happens. |
| 3.7 | **The Room Where the Union Was Argued** | Summer 1787 | Philadelphia | VIGNETTE | You're not in the Convention — you're in the taverns and print shops outside it, where a veteran's word carries weight. Debate vignette; the codex carries Federalist/Anti-Federalist sources. |
| 3.8 | **The First Citizen** | Apr 30, 1789 | Federal Hall, New York | CINEMATIC (finale) | The inauguration. As the oath is taken, the camera finds your character in the crowd, and the full memorial book — every soldier you lost, 1775–1787 — is the closing credits. |

## COMMAND MOMENTS (standalone historical-commander mode)

Separate from the campaign; play the pivotal hour as the actual commander,
with historical forces and a documentary frame:

1. **Prescott at the Redoubt** — Bunker Hill, Jun 17, 1775
2. **Washington's Gamble** — Trenton, Dec 26, 1776
3. **Arnold Unleashed** — Bemis Heights, Oct 7, 1777
4. **The Overmountain Men** — Kings Mountain, Oct 7, 1780
5. **Morgan's Trap** — Cowpens, Jan 17, 1781
6. **Hamilton at Redoubt 10** — Yorktown, Oct 14, 1781

## Accuracy commitments

- Dates, places, orders of battle, and outcomes follow the record; where
  sources conflict, the codex says so and cites both.
- The ugly parts are in: smallpox, slavery's contradictions (Dunmore's
  Proclamation, Black Loyalists' evacuation in 1783, Washington's own
  ledger), the frontier war's brutality (Sullivan Expedition treated as a
  somber narrative chapter, not a power fantasy), loyalist exile.
- No invented American victories, no invented atrocities, no cartoon
  villains: British and Hessian units get the same discipline, courage,
  and codex sourcing the Continentals do.
