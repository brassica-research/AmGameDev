# 04 — Characters

Casting rule: historical figures do what they did, when they did it, and
speak — wherever possible — words they are recorded to have said or
written. Invented dialogue is flagged in the codex as reconstruction.
Physical likenesses follow period portraiture (Peale, Trumbull, Stuart),
not modern actor-faces.

## The player character

**A created Continental officer** (player-named). Massachusetts militia
lieutenant, April 1775 → Continental captain (1776) → major (1777) →
lieutenant colonel commanding a brigade wing (1781). The composite-everyman
approach follows Joseph Plumb Martin's memoir: the war seen from the line,
not the map table. Background options (farmer, printer's devil, merchant
sailor, free Black freeman, frontier rifleman) tune stats and dialogue.
The PC never displaces a documented person from a documented act.

## The brigade (recurring fictional soldiers, historically typed)

A persistent supporting cast drawn from documented *types*, each carrying a
period-accurate biography: the 15-year-old fifer; the Marblehead fisherman
who can row anything; the veteran of the French war who's seen armies break
before; the freed Black private weighing Dunmore's offer against the
Patriot cause; the Irish redemptioner; the camp follower who runs the
mess and, at Monmouth, a cannon. Their arcs ride the historical systems —
enlistments, smallpox, Newburgh anger, Shays' desperation.

## Voice & portrayal standards

- Accents: regional-period, not modern-national caricature (Boston 1775
  did not sound like Boston 2026; British officers spanned Scots, Irish,
  and English regional voices).
- No character exists only to be wrong. Every faction's best argument is
  made by its most sympathetic figure — Dunmore's Proclamation is read by
  an enslaved man deciding, not by a mustache-twirler.
- Washington is not marble. He loses his temper (Kip's Bay, Monmouth —
  documented), doubts, and grows; the spectacles moment works because the
  game has let him be tired.
